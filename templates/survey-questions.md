# Survey questions

Send from one owner, one deadline five business days out, and say in the first line what will happen with the answers. Keep it under ten minutes.

## Who to ask

| Group | Who | Send as |
|---|---|---|
| Leadership | CEO, operations lead, finance | 6 questions by email, then a 30-minute call |
| Sales | Sales lead and every rep | Survey form, 10 minutes |
| Marketing and lifecycle | Whoever sends email and SMS; the agency that builds pages | Survey form plus a 30-minute call |
| Support and community | Support lead, shared inbox owners, program coordinators | Survey form |
| Builders | Everyone who wrote workflows, code or scripts | Builder questions by email, then a 45-minute call each |
| Outside parties | Ad agency, the contact-center vendor's success manager, any sales consultancy | 4 questions by email |

## Questions for every group

1. What do you do in HubSpot, the phone platform or the funnels in a normal week, step by step?
2. List every tool or app you log into in a normal week, including spreadsheets, personal tools and anything an agency runs for you.
3. What takes longer than it should? Name the task and roughly how long it takes.
4. What have you stopped trusting? A number, a report, a list, an automation. Why?
5. What broke in the last 90 days, how did you find out, and who fixed it?
6. What do you do by hand that you think a system should do?
7. What do you work around? Any spreadsheet, note or habit that exists because the system does not do something.
8. Which number do you report to someone else, and where do you get it?
9. If one person left tomorrow, what would stop working for you?
10. What have you asked for that never got built, and what did you do instead?
11. What is the one thing you would fix first?

## Extra questions by group

- **Sales:** Where do you see a lead first, HubSpot or the phone platform? Do deals and stages match what you did on the call? Has a bot ever texted into a conversation you were having? How do you know a sale was yours?
- **Marketing and lifecycle:** How do you build a send for an event today? What do you copy from the last one? What has gone out to the wrong list or with the wrong link? Which suppressions do you apply by hand?
- **Support and community:** Which questions come in every day that a page or a bot should answer? Where do replies go that nobody sees? What do you look up in two systems to answer one person?
- **Builders:** Which workflow are you afraid to touch and why? Which key or secret would you not know how to rotate? What did you build that only you understand? What is half-finished? What would you redo?
- **Leadership:** Which numbers do you make decisions on, and which of them have two versions? What did you expect the HubSpot move to fix that it has not? What is the cost of one wrong report?
- **Payments (ask finance, support and sales):** What types of payment do we accept or facilitate, and through what: card, bank transfer or ACH, buy now pay later, payment plans and instalments, PayPal or cash apps, invoices, wire, in person? Who sets up a payment plan and where is it tracked? How are comps and 100%-off codes issued and recorded? How are refunds, partial refunds and chargebacks handled, by whom, and where are they written down? Which products bill on a subscription, and which system bills them? Do affiliates or partners get paid, and from where? What payment has ever been taken outside the processor?
- **Outside parties:** What do you need from HubSpot that you get another way today? Which of our numbers disagree with yours, and which do you believe?

## From answers to findings

1. Day 1: send everything. Log every person asked and the date.
2. Days 2 to 5: one sheet, one row per answer: person, group, question, verbatim text.
3. Day 6: tag every row with a theme (data trust, manual work, broken automation, missing feature, handoff risk, vendor gap) and the step that can test it.
4. Days 6 to 8: 30 to 45-minute calls with the five most hands-on people. Ask them to show you the task on screen.
5. Days 8 to 10: each theme becomes a hypothesis in the risk register with the step that will test it.
6. During the assessment: confirmed, cleared or partly confirmed, with evidence. Nothing unconfirmed reaches the scorecard.
7. At the end: a two-paragraph note to every respondent on what their answers led to.
